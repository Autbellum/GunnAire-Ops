# GunnAire Ops Capability Audit

This audit compares GunnAire Ops with the practical operating capabilities described by ten leading field-service suites: ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, FieldPulse, FieldEdge, Jobber, Workiz, Service Fusion, Simpro, ServiceM8, and ServiceTrade. BuildOps was also consulted for commercial-HVAC workflow coverage. It distinguishes implemented workflows from vendor- or provider-dependent capabilities that cannot be represented honestly as live until the business has credentials, contracts, and production approval.

## Benchmark conclusion

Across the compared products, the consistent operational core is customer and equipment history; intake; drag-and-drop-style scheduling/dispatch; technician qualification and availability; estimates; approval; job execution; forms/photos/signatures; invoice/payment; agreements; inventory/pricebook; QuickBooks; communication; and reporting. GunnAire Ops implements that connected HVAC lifecycle with an iPad/Mac-first workspace and an intentionally narrower phone workflow for field staff.

Current differentiators and constraints are explicit:

- Dispatch now considers equipment qualification, open capacity, weekly workload, configured city/ZIP/territory cues, and dispatcher-managed breaks, training, time off, and unavailable periods. Dispatch-capable accounts also have a dedicated full-screen iPad/Mac week board: jobs can be dragged between days or moved with an accessible menu while preserving appointment time, rejecting crew/unavailability conflicts, protecting completed/cancelled work, and leaving Google-owned events read-only. Dense weeks scroll both horizontally and vertically, and every card opens its job editor directly so a rejected move can be resolved without leaving the board. Territory matching is advisory and never claims live GPS, traffic, or route optimization.
- Job documentation is now a focused lifecycle workspace rather than one overloaded list. Work contains HVAC readings, findings, equipment details, and workflow checks; Files contains equipment history, photos/imports, backend-shared files, and customer document generation; Billing contains estimate/invoice construction and linked documents; Closeout contains readiness and completion checklists. State-aware defaults take an unpaid linked invoice to Billing and paid/completed work to Closeout, while the explicit collection route continues directly to guarded invoice payment.
- QuickBooks Management is staged into Overview, Sales, Expenses, and Payments workspaces. Connection and sync health remain in Overview; customer/catalog/estimate/invoice work stays in Sales; vendor/bill/purchase work stays in Expenses; and payment-method/deposit/card operations stay in Payments. Sales includes an administrator recovery queue for every local invoice whose publication is pending or needs attention and an Admin-only review queue for field-created pricebook items. Estimate and invoice payloads carry the selected service property's address through QBO `ShipAddr`, while local import and duplicate reconciliation preserve the service-location ID and historical address snapshot. Pricebook approval records the reviewer and time, reconciles only a unique compatible QBO item, fails closed on ambiguity, and otherwise creates the configured QBO item. Invoice recovery rebuilds the complete QBO line set from the durable invoice snapshot, refreshes QBO's concurrency token before an update, and routes invoices with missing mappings or protected payment history back to Job Billing. Payments reconciles customer-scoped stored-card references only through an exact QBO customer ID or a unique customer-name match. The app persists only the opaque provider ID, brand, last four, expiration, active state, and reconciliation time; it does not persist a full card number, CVC, or one-time token, and it does not enable unattended recurring charges. Signed CloudEvents v1.0 change alerts are deduplicated and bound to the authorized company realm; the Overview shows only safe pending metadata and acknowledges the exact event set after a complete successful refresh. A persistent Sync action remains available without mixing all accounting resources into one long form. The Render entrypoint now imports the canonical tested backend rather than maintaining a second service copy, so production uses the encrypted, server-retained, realm/environment-bound refresh-token flow and the app receives only short-lived access tokens.
- Time tracking maps each technician to an explicit QBO Employee or Vendor ID instead of sharing one global worker reference. Clock-out stays durable locally, uses the job's QBO customer when available, embeds a stable GunnAire operation marker, and reconciles that marker before every create or retry. An uncertain lookup fails closed, while completed unsynced entries retain an actionable Retry control; this prevents a multi-technician company from posting labor to the wrong worker or duplicating time after a lost response.
- Payments is staged into Overview, Collect, and History. Overview shows balances and device/provider readiness; Collect contains assigned field tasks, unpaid invoices, customer/job handoffs, and guarded payment entry; History contains shared-company records, receipts, refunds, and QuickBooks follow-up. Field users land in Collect, and collection routes select that workspace before access and balance validation so failures are recoverable in context. Handoff shares only an invoice ID and is for nearby devices on the same Apple Account; the backend route supports separate staff accounts, permits one active assignee per invoice, attributes collection to authenticated identity, and advances partial or completed tasks from idempotent payment records.
- Receipts & Bills is staged into Documents, Purchasing, Inventory, and Recovery for administrators, while field accounts are constrained to Documents. Document capture and QBO linking no longer compete visually with supplier orders or stock movements; deferred/terminal upload evidence and retry controls remain together in Recovery. Deleting an entry or clearing the queue requires confirmation that automatic retry will stop without deleting the source file.
- Service-call detail is staged into Overview, Work, Billing, and History instead of one long mixed scroll. Scheduled and active jobs recommend Work, invoiced jobs with an open balance recommend Billing for Accounting and Admin accounts, and completed/cancelled jobs recommend History. Field, dispatcher, and standard accounts never receive the Billing lane, the current job-status action stays immediately below the workspace selector, and every Schedule day card has a distinct details control separate from edit and delete.
- Warranty and callback work now retains a durable bidirectional link between the original job and its scheduled corrective visit, including a concise reason such as workmanship, part failure, manufacturer warranty, unresolved concern, or customer courtesy. Schedule cards show one conditional callback/warranty badge instead of adding another dashboard, and authorized users can move between the original and corrective records from Overview. The new visit inherits customer/equipment/scheduling context but deliberately starts with empty readings and checklists so old field evidence cannot be mistaken for new work.
- Customer records are staged into Overview, Systems, Files, and History. Contact and consent maintenance, account health, open invoices, and customer actions remain in Overview; reusable service properties, installed equipment, maintenance agreements, and masked payment methods on file stay together in Systems; local and shared-company documents load only in Files; and job/email history stays in History. Each property preserves a stable identity, site contact, access instructions, primary/active state, and links to equipment and jobs. Jobs, standalone estimates, and invoices retain the stable property ID plus the address snapshot used for scheduling and customer documents; estimate approval can schedule work or convert directly to an invoice without falling back to the billing address. Deactivation removes a property from new-work selection without erasing historical context. The customer address remains the billing/default address rather than silently creating duplicate accounting customers. Stored payment references are visible only to financial roles and explicitly disclose that QuickBooks retains the payment credential. Directory rows use independent Open Record, Open Job, and Collect Payment controls rather than nested buttons. Standard and Accounting users receive an explicit read-only record, Dispatch and Admin can maintain operational customer data, only Admin can delete or push customers to QBO, and technicians use assigned-job customer context instead of the all-customer directory. Financial account context follows the Accounting/Admin billing-detail permission.
- Accounting and Admin accounts now have a dedicated Business Reports workspace rather than another Command Center section. Overview, Sales, Operations, and Team lanes share explicit month, prior-month, 90-day, and year-to-date periods and export the same snapshot to CSV. Invoiced revenue, collections net of refunds, open balances, estimate conversion, pipeline, job outcomes, corrective-visit rate, work mix, and technician hours are defined from their operational records. Gross profit and margin remain unavailable whenever a material cost, loaded labor rate, or completed-job time record is missing, so incomplete cost coverage cannot be presented as profit.
- Administrators have a compact Shared Server Readiness section in Settings → Sync. It verifies the authenticated production boundary, persistent data placement, SQLite read/write integrity, shared-file storage, encrypted QBO connection state, and recent locally verified backup evidence without disclosing paths, credentials, or customer content. The repository also includes manifest/hash verification and a non-destructive restore-drill utility plus an operator runbook; off-host scheduling and an operator-executed drill remain deployment duties rather than an in-app promise.
- Maintenance agreement visits retain a stable agreement ID and the exact obligation due date on the work order. Scheduling keeps the obligation due, reopens the existing job instead of creating a duplicate, and advances the cadence only after an authorized completion path. Customer Systems shows completed/open visit counts, and the job Overview identifies the agreement obligation without adding another top-level workspace. Agreement-management attention is limited to Dispatch/Admin; technicians continue to see only their assigned job cards.
- Invoiced non-inventory materials now hand off directly to a compact Job Materials ledger in the field Billing lane. The customer/QBO invoice snapshot remains the billing authority, while an assigned technician or administrator chooses the supplying truck or warehouse and records only the still-unaccounted quantity. Consumption fulfills the matching job reservation without releasing another job's allocation, returns remain traceable, and Closeout points back to Billing when an invoiced material still lacks a stock record. When use leaves the source below its reorder point, the same compact row creates one job/item-linked `Requested` replenishment record; Purchasing shows it for administrator review and can promote it to a supplier-backed draft without claiming an order was sent. Requested and draft records cannot receive stock. The job workflow no longer repeats the full back-office builder and customer editor beneath its compact controls.
- Staff can complete core field records offline and recover their work locally; CloudKit is the project-side replication path for company-owned iPad/Mac devices on the same approved business iCloud account.
- Provider-dependent tools—including live SMS, location tracking, financing, online payments, and supplier ordering—remain gated until the required commercial approval, credentials, consent, and test evidence exist. The safe iPad-to-iPhone payment handoff is built: for contactless collection it directs the field user to the matching QuickBooks Mobile or GoPayment invoice, because Intuit documents Tap to Pay in those apps rather than an embedded custom-app SDK.
- The primary navigation keeps frequent operations in the sidebar (Command Center, time, schedule, customers, documentation), while finance and integrations remain role-scoped back-office destinations. A native iPad/Mac **Navigate** menu provides Command-1 through Command-7 for the six most-used workspaces plus Business Reports. Every command uses the same pending-route and role-authorization path as the sidebar, so a hardware shortcut cannot reveal a restricted destination.
- Estimate follow-ups and invoice payment reminders now use a confirmed-send handoff instead of an optimistic status change. The draft carries the exact customer, job, estimate or invoice, and workflow type into Gmail. Only a successful provider response creates the communication record, changes the estimate/follow-up state, schedules the next office action, and adds a concise job activity. Cancelling a draft, using the unobservable `mailto:` fallback, a provider error, a paid invoice, or mismatched record context makes no operational change. Buttons say **Draft** until the user reaches Gmail's actual Send control.

## Implemented operational suite

| Capability | GunnAire Ops implementation |
| --- | --- |
| CRM and equipment history | Customer profile, contacts, equipment, serial/model/warranty/location, service history, files, photos, agreements, open work, and financial account signals. The customer record separates Overview, Systems, Files, and History so the full account remains traceable without presenting every editor and history list at once. |
| Field workflow | Scheduled jobs with a lead and additional crew, timestamped en-route and on-site handoffs, job-linked technician time, service/repair/replacement documentation, readings, checklists, reusable job-linked field forms, findings, attachments, follow-ups, warranty/callback/diagnosis classifications, corrective-work reasons and original-job lineage, no-access reschedule outcomes, and generated reports. A corrective visit inherits the customer, equipment, crew, and due-date context while starting fresh readings/actions; role checks also apply when opening either related job. Field files and form responses remain local and durable when the shared backend is unavailable; failed company-storage uploads receive a durable retry state and retry when the app next returns active with the backend configured. Lead and explicitly assigned additional-crew technicians receive the same guarded access to the linked job invoice and can collect only those assigned-job balances; unrelated invoices remain hidden. Approved office staff can also create auditable, server-authorized field collection tasks for active technicians; technicians can view and accept only their own active tasks, with a one-time in-app prompt when a newly assigned task is refreshed. |
| Lead intake and dispatch | Dispatcher/admin-only request intake, an optional consent-required and rate-limited online-booking inbox, explicit qualification, customer matching, auditable request-to-job conversion, and accepted-estimate-to-work-order scheduling. Dispatch/Admin choose a future appointment, work type, and duration in one focused sheet; the resulting job is explicitly unassigned for capacity validation, linked to the estimate, audited, and deduplicated unless the prior work was cancelled. The suite also includes calendar scheduling, a dedicated full-screen week board with drag and accessible-menu rescheduling, two-axis dense-schedule scrolling, direct job editing, lead plus crew assignment, crew-aware conflict checks, dispatcher-managed time off/break/training/unavailable blocks, distinct customer arrival windows that do not alter technician capacity, optional equipment-qualification profiles with an overridable mismatch cue, a durable job activity timeline for assignment, rescheduling, customer-window changes, status, billing, and payment handoffs, Google Calendar create/reschedule/delete handoffs limited to verifiably app-managed events, route handoffs, and dispatch/technician role restrictions. |
| Sales and billing | Good-Better-Best proposal sets with an explicit selected option; estimates and change orders with revision-scoped approval evidence; in-person drawn signatures; traceable email, text, or phone/verbal authorization references; staff-recorder attribution; customer PDFs containing approval fields, signatures, and the service address; and a durable accepted-estimate handoff to exactly one active scheduled work order. Standalone and job-linked estimates/invoices retain property identity and an address snapshot through scheduling, estimate-to-invoice conversion, PDFs, QBO `ShipAddr`, and QBO import/reconciliation. Service-type invoices, payment collection, receipt/bill workflow, QBO accounting handoffs, documented customer email delivery, and internal job costing combine selected-material cost with completed technician time at a configured loaded labor rate. A change order preserves the original accepted proposal and requires new evidence for the revised scope. Assigned technicians can amend line items and quantities on the linked unpaid draft; the app updates that same local/QBO invoice, persists publication state, fetches the current QBO concurrency token, and protects signed, finalized, or payment-bearing history from destructive edits. Administrators can recover failed or offline invoice publication from a centralized QBO Sales queue, while Command Center and customer intelligence include both invoice and payment sync risk. Labor cost is intentionally excluded from customer-facing documents. |
| Maintenance | Recurring maintenance agreements with plan terms, member visit pricing, included visits, covered equipment, due dates, renewal attention, staff-reviewed reminder drafts, and generated maintenance visits that preload covered-equipment technical context. Each generated visit carries durable agreement/due-date lineage, remains idempotent while open, advances the agreement only after completion, and contributes to a compact customer visit-history summary. Agreement scheduling is Dispatch/Admin-only. Recurring customer billing remains intentionally provider-gated until payment authorization and accounting reconciliation are approved. |
| Pricebook and procurement | Catalog/pricebook attention, vendor records, job-linked purchase orders, receiving status, cost traceability, and server-side vendor connector boundaries. Invoice-capable users can select existing catalog lines or create a service or non-inventory line inline, choose a quantity from 0.25 to 100 directly in the compact job builder, and keep that quantity in the durable document snapshot and complete QBO create/update payload. Field-created lines remain available on their job but require traceable Admin review before entering the company pricebook or QBO; approved items retain durable QBO pending/needs-attention/synced state and retry support. For physical materials, the saved invoice quantity drives a separate, role-scoped truck/warehouse use handoff; the job ledger records actor, time, source, reservation fulfillment, consumption, and returns without mutating the customer invoice or silently correcting stock. A below-reorder source can create one durable field request for the exact job/item; only an administrator can prepare its supplier-backed draft, mark it ordered, and receive it into stock. |
| Management controls and reporting | Google business sign-in, scoped roles, feature restrictions, job-level field-user access that limits field routes and deep links to assigned work, Command Center health queue, sync attention, company-server documents/communications, Apple privacy manifest, and an Admin-only server readiness view backed by server-enforced authorization. A separate Accounting/Admin Business Reports workspace provides focused Overview, Sales, Operations, and Team reporting, visible date-range definitions, CSV export, net-of-refund collections, and conservative job profitability that withholds profit/margin until material and labor coverage is complete. Manifest-backed backup verification, restore drills, and the operations runbook support deployment recovery evidence without claiming an off-host backup exists until the operator creates and retains one. |
| Customer communication | Gmail delivery, durable customer-facing delivery record, linked invoices/estimates/jobs/files, consent-gated appointment confirmation/on-my-way email drafts, customer-controlled operational/text/marketing preferences, administrator-created expiring customer portal links scoped to one appointment/invoice snapshot, and an explicit consent-gated post-job review-request email draft that requires an approved HTTPS review destination. Estimate follow-ups and payment reminders retain exact record context and update the job/estimate follow-up timeline only after Gmail confirms delivery; abandoned, failed, fallback, paid-invoice, and mismatched-record paths fail closed. |

## Provider-dependent capability gates

The following are designed as handoffs, not falsely marked live:

| Capability | Required before production activation |
| --- | --- |
| QuickBooks Online mutations | Intuit production app approval, server deployment, encrypted secret/token storage, realm authorization, accounting reconciliation acceptance, and configuration/testing of the Intuit-provided webhook verifier plus sandbox/production CloudEvents subscriptions. Signature, realm, deduplication, queue, and successful-sync acknowledgement are implemented. |
| Gmail/Calendar | Google OAuth production consent-screen verification, approved scopes, redirect configuration, user authorization, and organization policy approval. |
| Lennox/Johnstone purchasing/catalog | Supplier commercial onboarding, account authorization, approved API or punch-out credentials, pricing/branch entitlement, and order authorization policy. |
| Text notifications, automated review requests, financing, GPS/ETA | A selected provider, consent/data-retention design, server-side keys/webhooks, production error handling, and business approval. A staff-reviewed email draft is available for consented post-job review follow-up; automated sends are not enabled. |

## Deliberately sequenced next releases

1. Schedule verified backups to off-host storage, perform the documented restore drill, configure monitoring, and complete production role acceptance for the deployed authenticated HTTPS backend. The project-side readiness endpoint, verification utility, and runbook are implemented.
2. Complete QBO and Google production approval, then exercise only test-company/test-account flows before accounting or customer communications are enabled live.
3. Choose an SMS/booking/portal provider and implement its consent, opt-out, webhook, and delivery-retry lifecycle before presenting those as supported customer channels.
4. Complete supplier onboarding and add server-side adapter tests before allowing a purchase order to transmit to Lennox, Johnstone, or another supplier.
