# 1. Document intake and mechanical scope control

Inventory each file, page count, source fingerprint, title, project/location, issue date and revision/addenda status. Use the sheet index, not filename alone, to identify mechanical plans, demolition, details, schedules, controls and specifications. A five-sheet upload is not proof of a complete bid package. List missing references by their literal identifiers.

Inspect every mechanical page, including image-only tables, diagrams, photos and marginal notes. Extraction is a search aid, not a substitute for image review. Mark small or unreadable content for enlarged review. Do not count legends, title-block examples, key plans, schedule thumbnails, alternate details or enlarged repeats as additional equipment.

Build a scope matrix with:
- lifecycle: new; existing to remain; protect; demolish; abandon; relocate/reuse; reconnect; rebalance; repair/replace conditionally;
- trade/function: HVAC equipment, ductwork, ventilation, HVAC controls, HVAC piping, specialty equipment interface, plumbing, electrical or other;
- contracting role: furnish, install, remove, connect, startup, test, balance, train, permit, coordinate or closeout;
- applicability: included, excluded, conditional, by others or unresolved.

User defaults exclude plumbing and electrical, not every item located on a plumbing/electrical-looking detail. Pneumatic HVAC thermostat tubing is not dental compressed-air utility distribution. Dental equipment outdoor-air/exhaust lines are distinct from sanitary, water and dental suction/distribution systems. Record an unresolved trade assignment as an RFI and priced hold/explicit alternate, not a silent omission.

Honor shaded boundaries but look for explicit work keyed into retained areas: reconnect, rebalance or modify may remain mechanical scope even across an excluded-area line. Conversely, seeing equipment outside the work area does not authorize full replacement.

Extract mobilization, hours, site investigation, temporary protection, testing, shutdowns, permits, fees, coordination, access, patching, warranties and documentation obligations from M notes. Do not invent logistical requirements absent from the documents; distinguish a scope requirement from an unresolved condition or a proposed estimating allowance.

When fire ratings or structural information is missing, ask for the specific mechanical coordination/rating/penetration information needed. Do not expand into a full architectural, structural, plumbing or electrical takeoff without user approval. Where the mechanical documents impose a conflicting cross-trade obligation, explicitly obtain an agreed scope split; the user's requested exclusion is not represented as amending the contract by itself.
