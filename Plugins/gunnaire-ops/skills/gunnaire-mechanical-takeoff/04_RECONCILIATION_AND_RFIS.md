# 4. Reconciliation and questions

Run these checks before pricing and repeat after revisions:
1. Counts by type/lifecycle on primary plan versus ceiling/equipment views, with duplicate physical objects removed.
2. Every tag against its schedule: type, neck, size, finish, accessories and performance. Count may pass while specification fails.
3. Terminal airflows summed by traced branch/box; reconcile box totals and retained terminals. Do not compare total supply directly to undifferentiated return/exhaust as a universal equality: outdoor air, exhaust, transfer and pressure design need their own sourced balance.
4. Demolition removals versus retained/protected items and new connections; matching totals are not presumed.
5. Drawings versus general specifications versus manufacturer diagram/caption/model; no undocumented precedence is assumed.
6. Included scope versus explicit exclusions and M-note cross-trade assignments.
7. Every duct run/fitting/accessory and specification obligation accounted for by a priced line, approved allowance, explicit exclusion, RFI or not-applicable reason.
8. All sheet/detail references present; issue dates/addenda controlled; no unsupported claim of currentness.

RFIs contain ID, exact conflict/absence, both source references, requested decision, cost/schedule/procurement consequence, responsible recipient, status, written answer, responder and date. A verbal assumption is not an approved design correction. Keep the original question and source after resolution. A response that changes scope must trigger an estimate revision and corresponding proposal change.

Do not call a return grille opening and a smaller boot throat a definitive error without checking the intended geometry. Do not label contradictory paragraphs harmless boilerplate and erase them. Record project-name/asset-tag inconsistencies neutrally and ask applicability.

For each unresolved matter, choose one transparent handling method: Hold, Excluded pending agreed scope split, separately priced alternate, or clearly described approved allowance. Never use an unexplained contingency percentage as a substitute for identifying the missing work. A draft can be useful without being releasable.

Dental pilot regression cases: count 6 S1 + 17 S2 = 23 new supply; 21 R1 on each plan = 21 physical returns; eight existing VAVs are not eight new boxes; eight T symbols are not automatically eight confirmed relocation operations; two S1 locations have neck conflicts; 20 keynote-9 placements do not replace the note applying to all 21 return boots; 3,340 new + 425 retained = 3,765 CFM. Main-plan/caption conflicts and unresolved HVAC hydronic clauses remain open.
