# 3. Specifications, schedules, definitions and assemblies

Separate glossary/definitions from scope instructions, material requirements, conditional requirements and product data. A legend definition describes a symbol; it does not alone establish that every system in the job exists. Preserve a conflict between a legend and specific equipment notes.

For every relevant schedule row, extract the exact tag, basis manufacturer/model, service, dimensions, capacity/CFM, pressure/temperature where given, voltage/phase only for coordination, finish, mounting, accessories and footnotes. Read the whole row and every referenced footnote visually. Do not extrapolate installation requirements from a product-family name when the actual model or manual revision is unconfirmed.

Translate each explicit requirement into a requirement record linked to one or more takeoff lines. Record the exact source and applicability. Distinguish:
- mandatory present-project instructions;
- conditional instructions triggered only by a confirmed system, location or rating;
- apparently inconsistent/general clauses needing clarification;
- product information that constrains installation but does not by itself add new equipment;
- exclusions and work explicitly assigned to other trades.

Example supply assembly: approved diffuser model/tag; neck and face size; scheduled damper; insulated backpan; rigid/flex branch; required takeoff damper; clamps; supports; sealant; insulation and balancing. Include each accessory only where supported. Flag hardware already included in a quoted factory assembly to prevent duplicate pricing.

A return assembly has separate dimensions for the ceiling face, neck/opening, boot, riser/elbow and transfer throat. Different dimensions are not automatically contradictory; they can require a transition. Request fabrication geometry when missing rather than declaring a typo or purchasing a mismatched boot.

Match duct construction, material, insulation, sealing and hanger requirements to new, modified, exposed and concealed work separately. Do not extend a generic new-duct clause to full replacement of retained existing mains without project support. Preserve required acoustic, finish, service-access and substitution constraints. Do not replace a specified thickness with an invented R-value.

Manufacturer diagrams may contain equipment utilities outside the HVAC trade. Extract only relevant mechanical interfaces and log the rest as excluded or by others. Do not count pictured accessories as required simply because a diagram illustrates an optional arrangement. Captions, photographed labels and diagram model names must be reconciled before selection.

Track preconstruction surveys, tests/TAB, controls integration, filter changes, labeling, painting, permits, commissioning/owner instruction, record documents and warranty tasks even if they are not represented by plan symbols. These are cost-bearing requirements, not miscellaneous zero-cost notes.
